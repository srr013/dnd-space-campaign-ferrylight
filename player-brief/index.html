The Gloaming Skein
Player Brief

D&D 5.5e · three players · starting at level 5. Read this before you build a character.

The pitch

Worlds drift in a starlit dark, joined by old threads of light that nobody alive knows how to make. Parts of it are wonderful. Parts of it are civilized. None of it is as safe as it looks.

Who you are making

A native of the Sword Coast, level 5, built with the 2024 Player's Handbook. Any class, any core species. Something from outside the book is probably fine as well, just ask.

Session 0 begins at your players' homes on the Sword Coast. It does not end there. By its close you will be somewhere none of you has been, with no possible way back, and you will have to work out who you are there.

Don't get attached to your starting gear outside of essential items.

Skills you can learn

Out there, nine skills matter that nobody on the Sword Coast has heard of. Six are below, and the three flight skills (Piloting, Gunnery, Ship Systems) are covered under Crew roles. You won't have any of them at the start. Shortly after you arrive you will pick two, and you may get the chance to pick another later. A Training is a proficiency plus one small perk. If you already have the proficiency it names, you simply take the perk.

A Training gives you skill, not the object: a charge pistol costs 40 marks, and the crew starts with 150 to share. Much of a working ship has no gravity (cargo holds, engine rooms, airlocks, hull), and unanchored fighters in zero-g attack at disadvantage, so mobility and ranged options suffer less.

Crew roles

Some of the game happens aboard a ship, and a ship fight has three seats. There is no captain: the three of you decide together. Anyone can sit in any seat and nothing about your class locks you out, so you will choose seats at the table. Each seat has a few choices to make about how it fights, shown on a card you'll get later. Players will choose their ship proficiencies when they first fly, and they can switch their ship proficiencies when they level up, so while all players in the group should choose a unique proficiency you are not stuck with it.

Pilot

You fly the ship. Each round you pivot the ship to face a new direction, then move it in a straight line, always in that order. Easy turns are nearly automatic, sharp ones (a Hard Turn or a full About-Face) need a roll, and backing away with your guns still pointed at the enemy is harder than flying forward. If a roll fails you still do something, just less. Nothing carries over from one round to the next. Rolls use Vehicles (Space) (DEX or INT), plus your proficiency bonus if you've trained in Piloting.

Gunner

You fire the ship's weapons (d20 + DEX, plus your proficiency bonus if you've trained in Gunnery). You can only shoot what's inside your weapon's arc, so you and the Pilot are always talking.

Engineer

You run the power core. Each round you split its few points between Engines (speed and turning), Shields, and Weapons, and a point in one is a point not in the others. You also push systems past their limit and fix damaged ones, with a roll (Tinker's Tools or Arcana, INT, plus your proficiency bonus if you've trained in Ship Systems). Every other seat's best moment depends on a choice you made first.

On magic

Magic works exactly as the Player's Handbook says, and every class and spell is open to you. But it is rare out in the dark, and the people out there do not always welcome it. Some will be curious. Some will be frightened. Some will be worse than frightened. Casting in the open can bring trouble, so choose your spells, and your moments, with care.

Campaign Specifics

Ferrylight is a per-session newsletter that lists jobs, ship departures, and other news. You'll receive it digitally before each session

Money is essential to the completion of most tasks within the universe, and it will not be easy to come by. Humanoids you kill might drop money or loot that you can sell. You will have access to a jobs board with jobs you can complete. Jobs will have a time on them - you can complete up to 8 hours' of tasks per-day. Individuals may split up or complete tasks together; completing a task provides each person who completed it with the stated reward. All marks belong to the group: everyone earns into, and spends from, one shared pool, wherever each of you happens to be. The jobs on each session's Ferrylight are all the paid work there is, so once they're done, they're done unless the story offers more.

Time will be monitored, albeit loosely. You will spend days travelling, completing tasks, and exploring. Spending time on some activities means you will lose access to others. Look and listen for expiration dates.

Experience and levels will be based largely off certain accomplishments and time spent playing. Go off and conquer another world if you want - you may lose track of other threads but you'll still gain levels and wealth.

This campaign will have a few new rules to keep pace:

  • New rules were introduced to deal with space, and a Campaign Bible is maintained with current plans and rules as well as key outcomes from previous sessions. If no rule exists the DM will make one. The DM may reference the bible from time-to-time during sessions.
  • Jobs do not take in-game time. Once the group decides on a plan to complete Jobs they'll be considered completed and the clock moved forward.
  • Space flight will not take in-game time unless otherwise necessary to manage a situation.
